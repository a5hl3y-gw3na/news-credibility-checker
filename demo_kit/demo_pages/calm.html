Senate committee advances infrastructure funding bill

WASHINGTON - A U.S. Senate committee voted on Tuesday to advance a bill that would fund road, bridge and water projects over the next five years, according to a statement from the committee chairman's office.

The measure passed 14-8 in the committee and now goes to the full Senate, a spokesman for the Senate majority leader told reporters. Lawmakers said they expected a floor vote before the end of the month.

Senator Mark Hollis, who chairs the committee, said about 40 percent of the funds would go to repairs on existing highways, while the rest would be split between water systems and public transit.

Republican members said they supported the repairs but wanted tighter limits on spending, while Democratic members said the bill did not go far enough. Several members told reporters that talks would continue this week.

The Congressional Budget Office estimated that the bill would cost roughly $310 billion over five years, according to a report released on Monday. Officials at the Transportation Department said they had not yet reviewed the final text.

Governor Linda Reyes, speaking at a news conference in her state capital, said the funding would allow construction on a long-delayed bridge to begin next spring. A statement from her office said an independent audit would follow the first phase of work.

Business groups said they welcomed the vote, and a coalition of local officials said they wanted more detail on how projects would be selected. The committee said it would publish the full text of the bill on its website on Wednesday.
